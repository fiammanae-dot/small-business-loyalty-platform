Loyalty Card UAE WhatsApp Phase 2 10 Sep 2026

Phase 2 Without a Licence

Phase 1 is live and sending. The one-click path to onboarding clients is gated behind a trade licence you don't have yet — so this is the route that works in the meantime, and what changes when the licence exists.

The Partner shortcut is closed

Having a client share their WhatsApp Business Account with Loyalty Card UAE looked like a way around Tech Provider status. Meta's documentation rules it out on two counts:

Share your WABA (or any of your business assets) with any business-verified partner… The partner's app must have Advanced access for the whatsapp_business_management permission to access your shared WABA via the API. Without it, API calls return error code 200.

Both requirements trace back to business verification, which requires a trade licence. Partner sharing, Embedded Signup and Tech Provider status are all behind the same door.

What you can do anyway

Verification is not required to send real messages to real customers. An unverified business runs on the Cloud API with these ceilings:

250 unique customers per rolling 24 hours
2 business phone numbers per WABA
250 message templates per WABA
— no display name; chats show the phone number

A café enrolling thirty new customers a day sits at roughly a tenth of that ceiling. The binding constraint isn't volume — it's that the chat header shows a raw phone number instead of the business name, and that onboarding each client is manual work you perform rather than a button they press.

The onboarding model that works now

The key move: the client's business is the verified one, not yours. A licensed café in Dubai can verify itself. You operate inside their Meta portfolio as an admin they invite, rather than as an external partner business.

  1. Client Creates a Meta Business Portfolio, or uses the one they have

    Most businesses running Facebook or Instagram pages already have one.

  2. Client Adds you as an admin under People

    Person-level access inside their portfolio. This is what replaces partner sharing, and it needs nothing from Loyalty Card UAE.

  3. You Sets up their WhatsApp Business Account and registers their number

    The number must not be active in the WhatsApp Business app — expect this to be the most common snag with small businesses. Registration needs the /register call with a 6-digit PIN, the same step that fixed error 133010 on the test number.

  4. You Creates and submits their new_loyalty_card template

    Templates live per WABA, so every client needs their own copy approved. Body {{1}} customer, {{2}} business; URL button base https://loyaltycarduae.com/card/{{1}}.

  5. You Generates a system-user token in their portfolio and connects the channel

    Assets assigned first, then the token — assets before token, or the token comes out powerless.

  6. Client Verifies their own business with Meta

    Optional, and theirs to do. It's what turns the chat header from a phone number into their business name.

Worth deciding early

This model means you hold admin access to your clients' Meta portfolios and their access tokens. That's a real trust position and a real liability. Put it in writing in the service agreement — what you can access, what you'll never do with it, and how a client revokes it when they leave.

What to build

Ordered by what blocks the next client, not by size.

  • A connect form in business settingsFirst

    The WhatsApp panel is read-only today — it renders status and a test send, but has no inputs. Every channel so far was written by a one-off script. Add fields for phone number ID, WABA ID and access token, plus a save action that calls the existing encryptWhatsAppAccessToken. That function is written and tested but currently has no caller anywhere in the codebase.

  • Platform-admin scope

    Channel setup is your job, not the business owner's — the panel's own copy already says so. The form belongs behind platform-admin auth, with business owners keeping the read-only view they have now.

  • Template provisioning

    Create new_loyalty_card on a client's WABA through the API rather than by hand in WhatsApp Manager, and store the returned status. whatsapp_business_management covers this on their own WABA — no Advanced access needed when you're inside their portfolio.

  • Template status sync

    Today welcomeTemplateStatus is whatever was typed into the database. If Meta later rejects or pauses a template, nothing notices and sends fail one by one. Poll the template endpoint, or subscribe to the message_template_status_update webhook — the test console showed one firing already.

  • Delivery visibility

    Failures currently surface as a single lastErrorMessage on the channel. With several clients live you'll want the delivery queue visible in the platform admin, filtered by status, so a business quietly failing every send is noticed by you rather than reported by them.

  • Token expiry handling

    System-user tokens can be set to never expire, but they can be revoked — by the client removing your access, or by Meta. Decide what the panel shows when a token stops working, so it reads as "reconnect needed" rather than a raw Meta error code.

Carried over from Phase 1

Loose ends from the deployment that shouldn't get lost:

  • Rotate the committed secrets. TWO_FACTOR_KEY_FOR_VERCEL.txt, TWO_FACTOR_VALUE_ONLY.txt, WALLET_PRIVATE_KEY_FOR_VERCEL.txt and cookies.txt are in public GitHub history. Rotating the 2FA key forces enrolled admins to re-enrol, so plan that one.
  • Delete the scratch folder. _to_delete/ holds the production database password in plaintext, plus the WhatsApp token file. Gitignored now, but still on disk.
  • Separate local from production. Your local .env points DATABASE_URL at the production database. A Neon branch for development would remove a whole class of accident.
  • Commit the .gitignore change — it's still an uncommitted edit in the working tree.
  • Prove idempotency live when a second program exists: a customer joining their second programme at one business must not get a second welcome card. The unit test covers it; production hasn't exercised it.

When the licence exists

None of the above is wasted — the manual model and the Tech Provider model write the same rows into the same table. BusinessWhatsAppChannel already stores phoneNumberId, wabaId and an encrypted token per business, which is exactly what Embedded Signup returns.

  1. Days 1–10Submit business verification

    Trade licence as the primary document, address proof second. Set the region to United Arab Emirates first — it currently reads "Other". Meta quotes 2–10 business days.

  2. ThenOnboard as Tech Provider and submit App Review

    The recordings App Review wants are of a working send — which you now have.

  3. ThenSwap the connect form for Embedded Signup

    Same destination columns, different source. The manual form stays as the fallback for clients who can't complete the popup flow.

Onboarding is capped at 10 customers per 7-day window until verification, App Review and access verification are all complete, then rises to 200. Ten is more clients than the manual route would comfortably carry anyway.
