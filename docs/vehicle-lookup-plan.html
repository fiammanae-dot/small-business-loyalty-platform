Finding a Customer by Plate

A washer sees a car, not a phone number. If car washes are the first segment, plate lookup is what decides whether the app is faster than the paper card it replaces — and right now the platform has no idea vehicles exist.

Why this comes before milestone rewards

Al Bab Al Abyad's card has two customer fields: mobile number and vehicle number. They print both because the plate is how the card gets found at the counter.

The counter reality: a car pulls in, and often the driver isn't the account holder — it's the wife, the driver, the son. Asking "what's your mobile number?" while an engine idles is slower than reading the plate off the bonnet. Twenty times a day, that difference is the whole product experience.

An app that is slower than paper does not survive a pilot

Milestone rewards make the offer match what a business already runs. Plate lookup makes the daily use work. A car wash will happily live with one reward at nine stamps for a month. It will not live with a counter flow that costs an extra thirty seconds per car.

It is also the cheaper change: additive fields plus a search path, with no stamp-engine rewrite, no migration policy, and none of the seventeen inlined reward comparisons.

What a UAE plate is

DUBAI A 12345

Emirate · code · number — three parts, entered and searched separately.

Storing this as one free-text field is the mistake to avoid. People write the same plate as A12345, A 12345, Dubai A 12345 or dxb-a-12345, and a search that relies on matching those to each other will fail exactly when a queue is forming.

Three fields, one derived key:

// on BusinessCustomerMembership - per business, matching how phone is stored
vehicleEmirate  VehicleEmirate?  @map("vehicle_emirate")   // DUBAI, SHARJAH, ...
vehicleCode     String?          @map("vehicle_code")      // as entered: "A", "12"
vehicleNumber   String?          @map("vehicle_number")    // as entered: "12345"

// derived on write, indexed, never shown - the thing searches match on
normalizedPlate String?          @map("normalized_plate")  // "DUBAI-A-12345"

enum VehicleEmirate {
  ABU_DHABI  DUBAI  SHARJAH  AJMAN  UMM_AL_QUWAIN  RAS_AL_KHAIMAH  FUJAIRAH
}
The pattern already exists in your schema

This mirrors phone plus normalizedPhone exactly: keep what the user typed for display, derive a canonical form for matching, index the canonical one. No new idea to maintain — the same rule, applied to a second identifier.

Codes differ by emirate — some use letters, some numbers, and the valid sets have changed over the years. I would not hard-code them. Accept one or two alphanumeric characters, uppercase them, and let the emirate field carry the meaning. Validating against a fixed list risks rejecting a plate that is sitting in front of the washer, which is worse than accepting an odd one.

Where it lives, and why not on the shared customer

On BusinessCustomerMembership, not GlobalCustomer. Three reasons:

  • Tenant isolation. GlobalCustomer is shared identity across every business on the platform. A plate written there would be readable by whichever business touched that customer next. A coffee shop has no business knowing what its customer drives.
  • It is the business's own record. The car wash enters the plate, corrects it, and relies on it. That is per-business data, exactly like the notes field already there.
  • It matches the existing shape. firstName, phone and email are already duplicated onto the membership rather than read through the global record.

The cost is duplication if one car uses two car washes on the platform. That is the right trade: two businesses, two records, no leakage.

Show it only where it makes sense

BusinessType already has CAR_CARE_CENTER. Gate every vehicle field and the plate search on it, so a coffee shop never sees an empty "vehicle number" box on its enrollment form.

The data model stays general — a vet storing a pet name later reuses the same three-field shape. Only the UI is segment-specific, which is the cheap half to change.

What changes

  1. prisma/schema.prisma + migration 0047 Four nullable columns and one enum

    All nullable, all additive, no backfill. Index [businessId, normalizedPlate] — the search is always within one business, so the composite index is what actually gets used.

  2. src/lib/vehicles.ts (new) Parse, normalize, format

    One module, the shape of src/lib/phone.ts: normalizePlate() for storage, formatPlateDisplay() for the UI, and a parser that accepts what a washer would actually type into a single box. Pure functions, easy to test, no database.

  3. staff/customers + dashboard/customers Plate joins the search

    Both pages already match on name and normalizedPhone. Add a third clause on normalizedPlate, and try to parse the query as a plate the same way they already try to parse it as a phone. Typing 12345 should find the car.

  4. enrollment forms + customer detail Capture and display

    Emirate dropdown, code, number — on the owner, branch and public join forms. Shown on the customer record, and on the card, where it reassures the customer their car is the one on file.

  5. staff/scanner The flow that actually matters

    Plate as the primary way in: type the number, get the customer, stamp. This is the screen a washer uses twenty times a day, and the one worth watching someone use before calling it done.

Decisions before code

QuestionSuggested answer
Duplicate plates Warn, do not block. Cars get sold, and a family may share one. A hard unique constraint will eventually stop a real customer being enrolled while they wait.
Required? Optional in the schema, encouraged in the UI for car washes. A walk-in without their car should still be enrollable.
Editable later Yes. People change cars, and staff mistype. Worth an audit entry, since the plate is how the account is found.
Search partial numbers Yes on the number alone. A washer reading a dirty plate may only be sure of the digits — matching those and showing a short list beats an exact match that fails.

Suggested order

  1. Vehicle lookup

    Additive, no engine changes, makes the daily counter flow work. This document.

  2. Reward-check consolidation

    Route 17 inlined comparisons through the helper in rewards.ts. Behaviour-neutral, green tests, makes the next step a one-file change instead of a twelve-file one.

  3. Milestone rewards, Stage 1

    Schema, engine, redemption, card and Wallet. Nothing visible changes.

  4. Milestone rewards, Stage 2

    The editor that lets an owner put the discount at visit 2, 3 or 5 — whatever their card says.

With no deadline and one demo business, this is the cheapest moment to do all four. Every one of them gets more expensive once real customers are mid-card.
